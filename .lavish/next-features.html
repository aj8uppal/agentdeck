Convenience

Things that remove a trip to a terminal tab or an interruption.

When an agent stops on a permission or numbered question, the deck shows the terminal's own wording and its choices, and you press 1, 2 or 3. Today a blocked agent always costs a trip to its tab, because replies are deliberately refused while a question is on screen.

Cost and risk: this presses a key in an agent's terminal, so it gets the same care as replies did: the question shown must still be the one on screen at the moment of the key press, or it refuses. It would go through a Codex review round before you get it.

Snooze: tell one agent "not now" for 30 minutes or an hour. It leaves Needs you, ⌥⇧Space skips it, and it comes back by itself. Focus: hold hand-back banners for a set time while you work on one thing; blocked agents still get through, and everything held is waiting in the deck when the time is up.

What I would leave out

You asked for no bloat. These came up in the brainstorm and did not earn a place. Tell me if you disagree with any of them.

  • Charts and trends over weeks. Nice to look at, and they need storage and upkeep. The three readouts above answer the questions a chart would.
  • Cost in dollars. You are on subscriptions, so a dollar figure would be an invented number.
  • Send one message to many agents. Rare, and the worst kind of mistake to make quickly.
  • Automatic reply rules ("when it finishes, send X"). The queued reply already covers the case where you know the next step.
  • Notes and prompt templates. The Conversation tab already shows what you last asked; a template library is a second place to maintain.
